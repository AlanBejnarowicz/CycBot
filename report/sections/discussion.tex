\par Now we can test our \textbf{Hypothesis H1:} Participants will more likely rate well the~robot's emotions that involve the~ear movements (happy, sad) compared to emotions that involve only the~head movements (interested, annoyed).

\subsection{Method}

\par Ratings (Very Poor~(1) to Very Good~(5)) were treated as ordinal data. Two independent groups were formed based on the~type of emotions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Group~1 (ear movements): Sad and Happy,
  \item Group~2 (no ear movements): Interested and Annoyed.
\end{itemize}
For the~statistical test, the~one-tailed (Group~1 is more significant than Group~2) Mann-Whitney U-test was selected. It is a~non-parametric test suitable for comparing two independent groups with ordinal data. It does not assume normal distribution, making it appropriate for Likert scale data. Additionally, it is not sensitive for a~small sample size (22~samples). The~significance level was set to $\alpha = 0.05$.

\subsection{Result}
\[
U = 1122.0, \quad p \approx 0.187
\]
At $\alpha = 0.05$, $H_0$ ($p > 0.05$) cannot be rejected.

\subsection{Experiment's conclusion}

\par Hypothesis H1 is not supported --- there is no statistically significant evidence that emotions involving ear movements are rated higher than those with only head movements ($p = 0.187$).

\par The~experiment results implicate that added ear movements do not significantly enhance the~robot's emotional expressiveness. It can be caused by the~problems of the~robot's design and implementation. The~emotions are very basic and might not be easily recognizable by the~participants. Additionally, the~problems with the~robot's power source cause jerky motions, when the~servo and pumps are working simultaneously. It can distract the~participants and make it harder to~recognize the~emotions.

\par The~collected demographic data shows that the~responders are mostly male adults (average age 30.7~years) without any previous experience with robots. It might suggest that the~experiment results could be biased towards this demographic group.
